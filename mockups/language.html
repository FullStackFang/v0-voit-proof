The Gate.

Every application passes through a gate: a small piece of real work. Voit Proof draws that gate everywhere, in colour. Blue is proof, coral is the person, teal is the team. Warm cream, rounded tiles, big friendly type: a product people are glad to meet, not a test they are made to sit.

  1. 01Colour says who.Blue is proof, coral is the candidate, teal is the employer. The same five roles on every surface.
  2. 02Tiles, not cards.One rounded, tinted container, flat, never nested. It holds a figure, a number or a screen.
  3. 03The gate is the figure.Every diagram is strokes passing gates. The one in coral is the person you're looking at.
  4. 04Two grounds, one voice.Cream for reading, charcoal for statements. Sections alternate; roles keep their names on both.
  5. 05Counted things are mono.Numbers, code, timings, labels. Prose is friendly; evidence is exact. One pixel detail: the counter.

Ground · Charcoal
Statements.

Title, strategy, the big number. Warm, never black. Roles brighten; tints deepen.

The role rule.

Blue, coral and teal mean proof, candidate, employer, everywhere. A button is blue because it is the product's action, not because blue is pretty.

The one-coral rule.

On any figure, exactly one stroke is coral: the person you're looking at. Everyone else is noise or ink.

No neon, no black.

Chroma stays below 0.21. Neutrals are warm. Pure black and white are prohibited. Failing test output is the only red, and only inside an editor.

04 · The gate

Every figure is strokes passing gates.

A gate is a test. A stroke is an attempt. Colour says whose. This is the one drawing Voit Proof knows how to make, and it makes every diagram out of it: the embed, the score, the ranked pool.

Pass

The coral stroke goes through every gate and keeps going. A dot at the end: it arrived.

Not yet

The stroke stops at a gate with a bar. Calm, coral, no red. The next attempt starts from here.

Flag

A ring on the crossing: the résumé said one thing, the work said another. For a human to read, never an auto-reject.

Noise

The pile. Identical lilac strokes, all the same length, because they all say the same thing. One of them is Marcus.

Compared

How they worked, beside how strong developers worked: a faint ink bundle behind the coral stroke.

Ranked pool

Ordered by how far each got. Longest first, nobody removed. The team's view of the gate from the other side.

07 · Do and don't

What keeps it ours.

Clay's warmth and Handshake's rhythm, without becoming either.

Do
  • Give every colour a role before you use it.
  • Draw the gate. Strokes, gates, one coral.
  • Alternate cream and charcoal; let the band do the mood.
  • Put counted things in mono, 13px or larger.
  • Keep one action per screen as the ink pill.
  • End headlines with a full stop.
Don't
  • No neon, no gradients, no gradient text, no glass.
  • No nested tiles, no shadows, no side-stripe borders.
  • No 3D contraptions or stock illustration. Our figure is the gate.
  • No photos of candidates. People are strokes.
  • No red anywhere except a failing test inside the editor.
  • No em dashes. No "Oops".
