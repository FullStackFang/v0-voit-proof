# Voit Proof: the narrative spine

One beat per idea. Everything built should advance the through-line.
Written by the founder, 2026-10-05. Context for the product, not a deck to build.

## The through-line

Applying became free, so applications stopped meaning anything. Voit puts a small piece of real work back into every application, so the pool sorts itself by proof, recruiters start with the strongest real people, and candidates get something back for their effort.

## Slide by slide

1. **Title.** VOIT, with *Turn signal into substance* underneath.
2. **Thesis and ambition.** Two promises, one per side. Employers: the strongest real applicants rise to the top. Candidates: stand out on demonstrated skill. Ambition: reinvent how people apply, with every application backed by a verified record of real work.
3. **The employer and the fire.** Mid-size software companies hiring remote developers are buried under identical applications, and some of those applicants are organized fraud. Priya can't tell who's real or who's skilled. Beat: the problem is urgent and specific.
4. **The candidate and the frustration.** Marcus is good at the work, but his application looks like everyone else's, and he never hears back. Beat: the same broken system hurts honest candidates too.
5. **How it's adopted.** The embed diagram (below), then the buying roles. A recruiter pastes one line, and the engineering manager curates the challenges. Beat: adopting it costs almost nothing.
6. **The candidate's experience.** Applies as a guest, fixes one real bug in about 10 minutes, sees where he stands. Registering lets him track every challenge, get diagnostics, and practice. Beat: it's worth his time, not just a hurdle.
7. **Employer value proposition.** A pool reordered by real skill, résumé claims checked, nobody rejected automatically. Beat: from noise to a trustworthy shortlist.
8. **Candidate value proposition.** Judged on work, feedback that makes him better, and a record that grows with him. Beat: substance flows back to the person who did the work.
9. **Employer business model.** Free embed spreads; paid teams get custom challenges from their own code. Real numbers for price and cost. Beat: it pays.
10. **Candidate business model.** Free for candidates. Their attempts calibrate the challenges and build the network. Beat: the free side builds the asset.
11. **Strategy: Architectural.** We become the standard step inside applications and own what no single employer can build: the calibrated library, the attempt data, and candidates' records. AI is used narrowly. Beat: why it's defensible.
12. **Top 10 assumptions.** What has to be true, tested through conversations, pain first. Beat: how we find out if we're wrong.

**Appendix: what a candidate sees.** A failing test and code editor on the left; standing, feedback, and pooled reviewer reactions on the right.

## The embed diagram (slide 5)

Replaces the old four-step acquisition strip. Reads left to right:

1. **One line, any form.** A code chip showing `<voit-challenge role="full-stack"></voit-challenge>` pasted into an employer's application page.
2. **Candidate applies.** The challenge appears inline: fix one real bug in about 10 minutes.
3. **Scored on the work.** Tests pass or fail, and how they worked is compared with strong developers.
4. **Ranked pool.** The recruiter's applicant-tracking view, with the strongest real applicants at the top and nobody removed.
